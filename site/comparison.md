# Compare voting systems

Compare ballot formats, counting families, and criterion assessments side by side. Single-winner and committee criteria have different definitions, so their tables stay separate.

Choose **Pass** to require a criterion, **Fail** to find counterexamples to that guarantee, or another exact assessment. **Any assessment** adds no constraint. All active filters must match. Showing or hiding a column does not enable or disable its filter.

**Conditional is not pass. Unverified is not fail.** Open an assessment for its assumptions and source. These labels describe the documented rule and variant, not a score or a recommendation. Read the [single-winner definitions](/criteria.md), [committee definitions](/multi-criteria.md), and [source notes](/sources.md) before interpreting a comparison.

## How to use the comparison

- Search by method name, identifier, or basic idea. Narrow the results by ballot format or counting family.
- Open **Criteria: columns and filters** to choose visible columns and require exact assessments. For example, require both Condorcet winner = Pass and Monotonicity = Pass in the single-winner table.
- Sort by method name, counting family, or ballot format. There is no aggregate criterion score: guarantees are not interchangeable.
- Open **Rule and counting details** for a method's variant, tie policy, computation cost, and example limits. Follow its name to the full worked chapter.
- Scroll the matrix horizontally to see more columns. Switching between single-winner and committee tables clears criterion, family, and ballot selections because their meanings differ. Reset restores the starting view.

Without JavaScript, both complete tables remain readable with assessment notes and sources. Interactive filtering runs entirely in your browser and does not call the simulation API.

The [Wikipedia comparison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_electoral_systems) provides the coverage roadmap. This page uses this guide's maintained catalogs and qualified assessments, not a copied Wikipedia table. See also the [single-winner catalog](/methods.md) and [committee catalog](/multi-methods.md).
